# notifications module

Push, in-app messages, real-time publishing and sound levels (architecture §4.2). So far real-time publishing through Centrifugo, its connection tokens and its subscription proxy (architecture §6.1–6.2; RA §4.3; CU §4.3), and the panel's alerts (AP §7.3, §9.1.3).

## Rules

- **Centrifugo** holds the WebSocket connections; the API stays stateless (§6.1). Its channels are `infra/centrifugo/config.yaml`, mounted by the local `compose.yaml`, the tests (`src/centrifugo/test-centrifugo.ts`) and each environment's `infra/compose.yaml`; its secrets and subscription proxies come from the environment. A new namespace goes into that file, `NAMESPACE_PARTY` in `realtime-access.ts` and `channels.ts`.
- **Configuration** (`realtime-config.ts`), read at each call, each process what it uses: the API signs tokens with `CENTRIFUGO_TOKEN_SECRET` and its guard checks `CENTRIFUGO_PROXY_SECRET`; the API and the worker call Centrifugo's HTTP API with `CENTRIFUGO_API_URL` and `CENTRIFUGO_API_KEY`. Without them real time is off: the token routes answer `503 realtime_unavailable`, the proxy refuses every call, and the worker logs it and leaves the outbox as it is. In production a missing one stops the process at start (`requireRealtime`), since restaurant devices ring only through it (RA §4.3). No secret ships in an app (E7).
- **Connection tokens** (`realtime-tokens.ts`): `POST /v1/merchant/realtime/token`, `POST /v1/customer/realtime/token` and `POST /v1/captain/realtime/token` (`@AppSignedIn()`) and the panel's `POST /v1/admin/realtime/token` (`@SignedIn()`, its cookie on its own origin, so no secret is in the bundle) answer an HS256 JWT for 15 minutes (`CONNECTION_TOKEN_MINUTES`, as long as an access token). Its subject is the app or panel session, so Centrifugo's user is one device's or one browser's session; its `meta` is the party (`admin` for the panel) and the account, which Centrifugo passes only to the proxy (`include_connection_meta`). The app asks again when Centrifugo says it expired. A terminated restaurant's owners get `403 restaurant_terminated` (RA §5.5).
- **The subscription proxy** (`realtime-access.ts`): Centrifugo asks the API about every subscription, by namespace: `merchant` calls `POST /v1/merchant/realtime/subscribe`, `order` calls `POST /v1/customer/realtime/subscribe`, `captain` calls `POST /v1/captain/realtime/subscribe`, `ops` calls `POST /v1/admin/realtime/subscribe` (the proxies `merchant`, `customer`, `captain` and `admin`). The routes are `@RealtimeProxy()`: the guard compares `X-Centrifugo-Proxy-Secret` with `CENTRIFUGO_PROXY_SECRET` in constant time, so only Centrifugo calls them, and nginx answers them with 404 from outside. They stay out of the OpenAPI document (`@ApiExcludeEndpoint()`), so no client is generated; `src/http/openapi.test.ts` fails on any other route left out of the document, or a proxy route left in. The connection's session is checked as a request's is (`AppSessionsService.openSession`): an ended or unknown one is disconnected (`4501`, the client does not reconnect with it). The channel must be the session's own: `merchant:{restaurant_id}` the restaurant user's restaurant, unless it is terminated; `captain:{captain_id}` the captain's own; `order:{order_id}` the customer's order (checked by `ordering`, which registers it with `RealtimeAccess.allow`, since this module cannot read orders). A panel session is checked with `AdminSessionsService.openSession` (open, its account active, within its idle and maximum ends, without recording activity, so an open socket never keeps an idle session alive); `ops:city.{city_id}` takes a user who sees the board (`operations.read`) or receives alerts (OWN, OPS, FIN; `readsOps`, owner decision 2026-11-08); every user has every city in V1. Anything else is refused with `403`.
- **The captain channel** (CA §15, CA §4.9): the outbox relay publishes an order's event to `captain:{captain_id}` of the captain it is about (an offer's, or the one assigned). A captain's sign-in ends the other devices' sessions inside its transaction and calls `notifyEnded` after it commits, so their connections end.
- **Sessions that end** (`realtime-sessions.ts`, CU §5.4, RA §5.1): `AppSessionsService` tells its `onEnded` listeners once a sign-out, a sign-out everywhere or a refresh reuse commits (`AdminSessionsService` once a panel sign-out commits), and this module disconnects each session's connections through Centrifugo's `disconnect` API, without making the sign-out wait or fail. A session ended inside another module's transaction (`endAll`) is disconnected when that module calls `notifyEnded` after its commit.
- **Calling Centrifugo** (`realtime-publisher.ts`): `broadcast` (with an idempotency key, so a repeat within a few minutes is dropped) and `disconnectSession`, 2 s each. A failure throws `PublishFailedError`: `answered` when Centrifugo refused the call, not when it was out of reach, failed (any HTTP error status) or answered something unreadable. The outbox relay in `ordering` and this module's alerts relay are the only publishers; the worker gets it from `NotificationsWorkerModule`.
- **History and recovery** (§6.1): each namespace keeps recent publications (`history_size`, `history_ttl` 10 minutes) and the apps' force recovery, so a client that reconnects with its offset gets what it missed; when it cannot, it fetches the full state (RA §4.3). `ops` forces none: the panel fetches the full state after every subscription (§10.3). Centrifugo's engine is memory, so a Centrifugo restart loses history, and clients fetch the full state.
- **The ops channel** (`ops:city.{city_id}`, §6.2, §10.3; owner decision 2026-11-08: every order event and every alert change): the orders' relay publishes each order event there (`ordering`'s `outbox-relay.ts`), and every alert raised, acknowledged or resolved is a row of `notifications.alert_outbox` written by `AlertsService` in the change's own transaction (E4; the application role inserts and may change only `published_at`). The worker's `AlertsRelay` (`AlertsWorkerModule`) publishes them every `ALERTS_RELAY_MS` (1 s; many modules' transactions write them, so a poll of the unpublished index wakes it rather than a job in each), as the orders' relay does: id order, `FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED`, idempotency key `alert-outbox:<id>`; an alert of the whole platform goes to every active city. An alert's publication is its type, the alert, its code, severity and time; the panel reads the list again, which answers what its roles receive. Browsers connect only from the panel's origin (`CENTRIFUGO_CLIENT_ALLOWED_ORIGINS` in each compose file).
- **Alerts (AP §7.3, §9.1.3; owner decisions 2026-11-07: slice 2.2 in two pull requests):** `notifications.alerts`, one row per alert, kept after it closes as the history. `alert-rules.ts` is the catalogue built so far: each rule's code, severity, recipient roles, subject (`order`, `captain`, `otp_pool`, `otp_number`) and whether it resolves itself; each later alert joins it with its slice. The module that owns a condition calls `AlertsService.sync(executor, subject, evaluated, open, now)` in its own transaction: it raises the codes of `open` not open for the subject and resolves the open ones of `evaluated` no longer in `open`. A partial unique index keeps one open alert per rule and subject, so two syncs at once leave one. A rule whose condition never clears (`away_from_address`, information) is raised once per subject and closes with its acknowledgment (an arrival away and a delivery away on one order are one alert). `GET /v1/admin/alerts?cityId=&state=open|closed` (`@SignedIn()`): the open alerts of the city and of the whole platform (`city_id` null) that the session's roles receive, by severity then age, or the latest 100 closed; `POST /v1/admin/alerts/{alertId}/acknowledge` («استلام», `@SignedIn()`) assigns it to the first recipient who takes it; a later call answers it with who took it, so it needs no `Idempotency-Key`. An alert the roles do not receive is `404`. The answer names who took it (`AdminSessionsService.names`).
- **Who syncs what:** `ordering` syncs an order's alerts (`order-alerts.ts` there); the worker's `AlertsSweep` (`AlertsWorkerModule`, every `ALERTS_SWEEP_MS`, 30 s) reads what no transaction of ours changes: the code senders' health (`OtpSenderPoolService.read`, through `OtpSenderPoolModule`; skipped in development, whose codes go to stdout with no number) for «لا يمكن إرسال رموز الدخول» and «رقم إرسال الرموز متوقف», and each city's captains at their cash limit (`CaptainsService.cashLimits`, at current rates) for «كابتن بلغ حد العهدة». A subject it no longer sees (a number disabled, a captain terminated) has its alert resolved; a captain whose cash cannot be read keeps its alert as it was.
- The proof is `src/modules/ordering/order-realtime.test.ts`: a real Centrifugo, the API on a real port behind its proxy, and `centrifuge` clients.

## Not yet

- `customer:` and `ops_map:` channels (§6.2), with their first publishers; tickets, the cash desk and the emergency stop on `ops:` with their slices.
- A panel session ended by `endAll` (force sign-out, disable, TOTP reset) keeps its connection until its token's expiry (15 minutes) or its next subscription; its events name ids only.
- Cities per role (the city scope of an admin account): `readsOps` lets a user into every city.
- The other alerts of AP §9.1.3 (tickets, edits, item unavailable, the captain's problem and emergency, the customer not answering, the restaurant auto-paused or without a device, settlement, approvals, applications, probation, documents, acceptance rate, stale rates, price changes), with their slices.
- The termination of a restaurant does not end its sessions, so its devices' connections stop at their token's expiry (15 minutes); the termination's slice disconnects them.
- Presence and the restaurant devices' heartbeats (RA §4.3, §4.6, architecture §6.3), owner decision 2026-11-06: later, with the receiving-device rule. `infra/centrifugo/config.yaml` sets no `client.ping_interval` yet (Centrifugo's default is 25 s, the spec's heartbeat 20 s and disconnect 60 s): set it with that work. The connection's user being the session makes presence per device.
- Push (FCM, APNs), owner decision 2026-11-06: later; the outbox relay is where it joins, and AP §8.3's apology on an automatic cancellation with it.
- A Valkey engine for Centrifugo, so history survives a restart and several Centrifugo nodes share it.
